Outliers, for programmers

Mistakes versus rare cases, classic and robust univariate detectors (and why the classic z has a ceiling), masking, Mahalanobis distance with robust covariance, Isolation Forest, influence and Cook's distance, and robust regression with Huber and Theil-Sen, along with their breakdown points. With a from-scratch module verified against SciPy, statsmodels and scikit-learn, a comparison on 800 trips, and a worked example by hand.

Summary

  • A detector only ranks points by "unusualness". Deciding whether an unusual point is a mistake requires domain context.
  • The mean, the standard deviation and least squares have a breakdown point of 0. The median and the MAD, 50%; Theil-Sen, 29%.
  • The classic z is bounded by and suffers from masking. Use the robust z (median and MAD) or Tukey's fences.
  • Many mistakes only show up in the combination of columns: residuals of a robust model, Mahalanobis with MCD, or Isolation Forest.
  • Huber limits the influence of large residuals but not that of high-leverage points; Theil-Sen and MM estimators do.

§1 · What an outlier is

Contamination, novelty and rare cases

Hawkins (1980): "an observation which deviates so much from the other observations as to arouse suspicions that it was generated by a different mechanism". The nuance matters: the different mechanism can be

  • A mistake (typo, units, sensor): it should be fixed or treated as a missing value.
  • An underrepresented subpopulation (mountain routes, refrigerated trucks): it is real and the model should learn it, usually with a feature that identifies it.
  • What you are looking for (fraud, breakdown): the outlier is the signal.

A useful model is Huber's contamination model: the data come from , with the process of interest and arbitrary. A robust estimator gives results close to those of for small , whatever is. The breakdown point is the largest it can withstand without being driven to an arbitrary value.

The simulated fleet (800 trips) has 12 mistakes (4 misplaced decimals, 3 in gallons, 5 misreported loads) and 31 rare but real trips (24 mountain, 7 refrigerated).

§2 · One column

Classic z, robust z and Tukey's fences

The classic z has two flaws. The first is structural: by Samuelson's inequality,

so with the rule never flags anything. The second is masking: several outliers inflate and shift , so they stop looking far away. (The opposite effect, swamping, is when outliers shift the mean so much that good points look like outliers.)

The robust z (Iglewicz and Hoaglin) swaps the mean and standard deviation for the median and the scaled MAD:

with a usual threshold of 3.5. Tukey's fences flag whatever falls outside , with ("outlier") or ("far out"). For a normal distribution, leaves out 0.7% of the data. With skewed distributions (distances, durations) it is better to apply the fences to the logarithm or to use the skewness-adjusted version (medcouple).

For time series, the Hampel filter applies the robust z over a moving window: each value is compared with the median and MAD of its neighbors.

§3 · Several columns

Robust residuals, Mahalanobis and MCD

A 20 t load and a fuel use of 24 L are normal on their own; together, impossible. Three ways to detect it:

  • Residual of a robust model: if one feature can be predicted from the others, fit a robust model and apply the robust z to the residuals. It is the most interpretable.
  • Mahalanobis distance: , which follows a for normal data. With the classic it suffers from masking just like z. The robust version uses the minimum covariance determinant (MCD): the subset of points whose covariance has the smallest determinant (MinCovDet in scikit-learn, FAST-MCD algorithm).
  • Isolation Forest (§4): assumes no shape for the data.

§4 · Isolating

Isolation Forest

The idea of Liu, Ting and Zhou (2008): outliers are few and different, so they get isolated with fewer cuts. An isolation tree takes a subsample of points (256 by default) and splits it recursively, choosing a random feature and a uniform threshold between its minimum and maximum, until each point is isolated or the depth reaches . The path length is normalized by the average length of an unsuccessful search in a binary search tree:

: isolated right away, an outlier. : normal. When a leaf ends with points not yet isolated (because of the maximum depth), is added to the depth reached. scikit-learn returns in score_samples and a threshold set by contamination.

Strengths: linear in , no distances, works in many dimensions. Weaknesses: axis-parallel cuts (the Extended Isolation Forest variant uses oblique hyperplanes), and the score doesn't say why a point is unusual.

§5 · Influence

Leverage, residuals and Cook's distance

In a linear regression, the hat matrix gives the fitted values . Its diagonal (the leverage, between and 1) measures how much point can pull on its own fit: it is high if is far from the center of the values. Cook's distance combines leverage and residual:

and measures how much all the predictions change when point is removed. People usually watch for . Careful: is estimated with the outlier itself included, so also suffers from masking. In the example in §10, the trip with 330 L has , below the classic threshold , even though it bends the line completely.

Telling apart the two kinds of outlier in regression is key: in the response (unusual with normal , like the misplaced decimal) and in the features (unusual , high leverage, like the misreported load).

§6 · Fitting without deleting

Robust regression: Huber, Theil-Sen and their breakdown

M-estimators replace the least-squares square with a function that grows more slowly. Differentiating, each point enters with a weight , and it is solved by iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS):

With Huber (: 95% efficiency if the errors are normal), a huge residual weighs little, but the influence of a high-leverage point is not bounded: a point with an extreme can pull the line toward itself and end up with a small residual, and its weight goes back to 1. Its breakdown point is 0.

Theil-Sen takes the median of the pairwise slopes: breakdown point and cost (there are versions). MM estimators and RANSAC reach 50%. The widget compares the three against misreported loads (leverage outliers).

§7 · From scratch

A verified outlier module

outl.py

import numpy as np

Q75 = 0.6744897501960817            # 0.75 quantile of the standard normal

def z_clasico(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, float); return (x - x.mean()) / x.std(ddof=1)

def mad(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, float); return np.median(np.abs(x - np.median(x)))

def z_robusto(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, float); return (x - np.median(x)) / (mad(x) / Q75)

def vallas_iqr(x, k=1.5):
    q1, q3 = np.percentile(x, [25, 75]); r = q3 - q1
    return q1 - k * r, q3 + k * r

def ols(X, y):
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones(len(X)), X]); return np.linalg.lstsq(A, y, rcond=None)[0]

def huber_irls(X, y, t=1.345, tol=1e-10, max_iter=200):
    """Huber via IRLS; scale = median(|residuals|) / 0.6745 at each iteration (like statsmodels)."""
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones(len(X)), np.asarray(X, float)]); y = np.asarray(y, float)
    b = np.linalg.lstsq(A, y, rcond=None)[0]
    for _ in range(max_iter):
        r = y - A @ b; s = np.median(np.abs(r)) / Q75
        u = np.abs(r / s); w = np.where(u <= t, 1.0, t / np.maximum(u, 1e-300))
        sw = np.sqrt(w); b_new = np.linalg.lstsq(A * sw[:, None], y * sw, rcond=None)[0]
        if np.max(np.abs(b_new - b)) < tol: b = b_new; break
        b = b_new
    return b, w

def cook(X, y):
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones(len(X)), np.asarray(X, float)]); y = np.asarray(y, float); n, p = A.shape
    H = A @ np.linalg.pinv(A.T @ A) @ A.T; h = np.diag(H)
    e = y - H @ y; s2 = e @ e / (n - p)
    return e**2 / (p * s2) * h / (1 - h) ** 2, h

def mahalanobis(X, centro, cov):
    D = np.asarray(X, float) - centro; return np.sqrt(np.einsum("ij,jk,ik->i", D, np.linalg.inv(cov), D))

def c_n(n):
    n = np.asarray(n, float); m = np.maximum(n, 3)
    return np.where(n > 2, 2 * (np.log(m - 1) + np.euler_gamma) - 2 * (m - 1) / m, np.where(n == 2, 1.0, 0.0))

def _arbol(X, rng, prof, lim):
    if prof >= lim or len(X) <= 1 or np.all(X.max(0) == X.min(0)):
        return ("hoja", len(X))
    j = rng.choice(np.where(X.max(0) > X.min(0))[0]); c = rng.uniform(X[:, j].min(), X[:, j].max())
    m = X[:, j] < c
    return ("nodo", j, c, _arbol(X[m], rng, prof + 1, lim), _arbol(X[~m], rng, prof + 1, lim))

def _camino(x, nodo, prof=0):
    if nodo[0] == "hoja": return prof + float(c_n(nodo[1]))
    _, j, c, iz, de = nodo
    return _camino(x, iz if x[j] < c else de, prof + 1)

def isolation_forest(X, n_arboles=200, muestra=256, seed=0):
    X = np.asarray(X, float); rng = np.random.default_rng(seed); psi = min(muestra, len(X))
    lim = int(np.ceil(np.log2(psi))); h = np.zeros(len(X))
    for _ in range(n_arboles):
        t = _arbol(X[rng.choice(len(X), psi, replace=False)], rng, 0, lim)
        h += np.array([_camino(x, t) for x in X])
    return 2 ** (-(h / n_arboles) / float(c_n(psi)))

§8 · On the fleet

Five detectors against mistakes and rare cases

import numpy as np, pandas as pd
from sim import viajes                       # simulator (included in the download)

lo, hi = vallas_iqr(y)
b, w = huber_irls(x, y); r = y - (b[0] + b[1] * x); s = np.median(np.abs(r)) / Q75

           "Isolation Forest 4%": ifo >= np.quantile(ifo, 0.96)}
for k, m in metodos.items():

# robust residual > 3.5  flagged  26  mistakes 12/12  rare 14/31  normal  0

The single-column detectors find the misplaced decimals (and the fences also catch the gallons), but none of them sees the misreported loads. The robust residual finds all 12 mistakes without a single false alarm among the normal trips, plus 14 mountain trips: rare but real. Isolation Forest, not knowing that fuel use depends on load, flags normal trips at the edges of the cloud (very low or very high loads). When there is a known relationship between columns, the residual of a robust model is usually the best detector and the easiest to explain.

§9 · Checklist

Before removing or keeping an outlier

  • Detection with robust estimators (median, MAD, quartiles), never with the mean and standard deviation.
  • Multivariate search: residuals of a robust model, Mahalanobis with MCD, or Isolation Forest.
  • Each flagged point reviewed with context: is it physically possible? Is there a column that explains it?
  • Mistakes with a known cause, fixed (units, decimal point); unrecoverable ones, as missing values.
  • Real rare cases, kept, with a feature that identifies them.
  • If they can't be reviewed: a robust loss (Huber, Theil-Sen, MM) or, for trees, an absolute or Huber loss on the target.
  • The rules, turned into code and applied the same way in training and in production (without looking at the test set).

§10 · Worked example

A decimal point that quadruples the slope

Six trips and all the math by hand: least squares with and without the mistake, the ceiling of the classic z, robust z, Tukey's fences, and the Theil-Sen line.

The setup. A fleet manager wants to know how much fuel use goes up per ton, to budget a contract with 20 t loads. They have six test trips, one of them copied by hand with the decimal point in the wrong place.

Example map

Six trips with their load and fuel use; in one of them, the decimal point is in the wrong place. We compute by hand the ordinary least-squares line with and without that trip, check which detectors catch it (the classic z can't, for a curious reason), and compute a robust line, Theil-Sen, that doesn't get dragged along.

    Where do these numbers come from?

    They follow the relationship of the simulated fleet: about 22 L/100 km as a base and 0.9 L more per ton, with a bit of noise. Trip F actually used 33.0 L; when it was copied, someone wrote 330. We know because this is an example; with real data you would have to find out.

  2. The ordinary line with the wrong trip

    In words

    The least-squares slope is the sum of the products of deviations divided by the sum of the squared load deviations. Then the line goes through the mean point.

    The math (means: 10.33 t and 80.67 L)

    What it means: the line says each ton adds 3.9 L/100 km, more than four times the real value, and that an empty truck burns 40 L. Both are false, and both come from a single trip. F contributes of the 638.7 in the sum of products: on its own it outweighs the other five combined.

  3. The ordinary line without the wrong trip

    The math (five trips; means: 10 t and 30.8 L)

    What it means: 0.875 L per ton and a 22 L base, very close to the real relationship (0.9 and 22).

  4. Does the classic z catch it?

    In words

    We compute how many standard deviations F is from the mean fuel use.

    The math

    Why doesn't a value 10 times bigger than the rest get past 3?

    Because with data points, the classic z cannot exceed . With 6 points, that ceiling is , exactly what F gets: it is the extreme case and it still doesn't reach 3. The wrong value itself inflates the mean and the deviation it is measured against. With 11 points the ceiling is 3.02; below that, the "z > 3" rule can't flag anything.

    What it means: the classic z is useless with small samples and works poorly with many outliers (masking, idea 3 of the simple version).

  5. Robust z and fences

    In words

    We replace the mean and standard deviation with the median and the MAD (the median of the distances to the median), which the wrong value can't drag along.

    The math

    Fences with k = 1.5 (quartiles by linear interpolation)

    Where do 28 and 37 come from?

    With 6 sorted values, the 0.25 quantile sits at position : between the 2nd (27) and the 3rd (31), a quarter of the way along, . The 0.75 quantile sits at position 4.75: . It is the rule used by numpy, pandas and Excel (QUARTILE.INC).

    What it means: both robust methods flag F without hesitation (36.5 against the 3.5 threshold; 330 far above 50.5) and flag no other trip.

  6. The Theil-Sen line

    In words

    We compute the slope between every pair of trips (15 pairs) and keep the median. The intercept is the median of . A wrong point only contaminates the 5 pairs it takes part in: not enough to move the median of 15.

    The 15 slopes, sorted

    Where do −148 and 149.5 come from?

    They are the pairs of F with its neighbors in load: with C (10 t), ; with D (14 t), . F's 5 slopes end up at the extremes (two very negative, three very positive), and the median, the 8th, still comes from the good pairs. Theil-Sen withstands up to 29% wrong points.     The math (ordinary line with F, ordinary line without F, and Theil-Sen with F)

    What it means: the ordinary line with the mistake predicts 118 L/100 km, which is absurd. The robust one, computed with the mistake included, gives practically the same as the ordinary line without it. And the robust z says exactly which trip to review: fixing it to 33.0 recovers the value without losing the trip.

import numpy as np
from outl import z_clasico, z_robusto, vallas_iqr, ols, mad

x = np.array([2, 6, 10, 14, 18, 12.]); y = np.array([24, 27, 31, 34, 38, 330.])
print(ols(x, y).round(2), ols(x[:5], y[:5]).round(3))                 # [40.26  3.91] [22.05  0.875]
print(round(z_clasico(y)[5], 2), round(5 / np.sqrt(6), 2))             # 2.04 2.04  (the ceiling)
print(np.median(y), mad(y), round(z_robusto(y)[5], 1))                 # 32.5 5.5 36.5
print(tuple(map(float, vallas_iqr(y))))                                                 # (14.5, 50.5)
i, j = np.triu_indices(6, 1)
b1 = np.median((y[j] - y[i]) / (x[j] - x[i])); b0 = np.median(y - b1 * x)
print(b1, b0)                                                          # 0.875 22.25
for b in (ols(x, y), ols(x[:5], y[:5]), (b0, b1)):
    print(round(b[0] + 20 * b[1], 2))                                  # 118.47 39.55 39.75

What would change in a real system

  • More trips: with hundreds, the robust z on the residuals (not on fuel use) also finds the load mistakes.
  • More features (route, refrigerated, grade): without them, mountain trips show up as outliers.
  • Fix before deleting: trip F is a valid trip with 33.0 L.
  • Validate on the test set that the cleaning rule doesn't change the model's error on data it hasn't seen (topic 1).

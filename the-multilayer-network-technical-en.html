The multilayer network, for programmers

The multilayer perceptron (MLP) as a composition of affine layers and activations: matrix shapes and batched computation, the geometry of a hidden layer (half-planes combined with logic gates, XOR and the band), the hidden space where the data become linearly separable, parameter counting, the universal approximation theorem read carefully, and why depth multiplies the linear regions. With a from-scratch class verified against PyTorch and against MLPClassifier, and a 2-2-1 network worked out by hand.

Summary

  • A layer of neurons over inputs is and ; for a batch , . It has parameters.
  • Each hidden neuron defines a hyperplane; the output neuron combines their half-spaces like a soft logic gate (OR, AND, “A and not B”). Two hidden neurons are enough for XOR and for the band of the 30 trips from topic 1 (6 mistakes with one line, 0 with the 2-2-1 network).
  • The hidden layer is a change of representation : in the hidden space, the output is a logistic regression and the data are linearly separable.
  • Universal approximation (Cybenko 1989, Hornik 1991, Leshno et al. 1993): with one hidden layer with a non-polynomial activation and enough width, there is a network within of any continuous function on a compact set. It is an existence theorem: it doesn’t bound the width or say training will find it.
  • Depth composes folds: layers of 2 ReLUs give linear pieces with parameters; a single layer needs neurons.

print("mistakes:", int(np.sum((P[:, 0] > 0.5) != y)), "of", len(y))

# the same with the module's class, and the parameter count
red = MLP([Densa(W1, b1), Densa(w2, b2)])
print("MLP:", red.tamanos, "parameters:", red.n_param, " max |difference| =", np.max(np.abs(red.forward(X) - P)))

§2 · Geometry

Half-planes and logic gates: XOR and the band

With a step activation, hidden neuron equals 1 on the half-space . The output neuron receives a binary vector and is itself a linear threshold, so it can implement any linearly separable Boolean function of the :

The positive region of a network with one hidden layer of steps is therefore a combination of half-spaces: with OR, a union; with AND, an intersection (a convex polygon if it takes all of them). XOR, which no single neuron separates (topic 1, §7), comes out with , and the output : the positive region is the band between two parallel lines. That is how Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams (1986) presented it when they showed that hidden units learn representations a perceptron doesn’t have.

The band of the 30 trips is the complementary problem: normal lies between the lines and (load , fuel use ), and odd lies outside, so the output is an OR of “above the upper line” and “below the lower line”. With sigmoids, each half-space becomes a smooth ramp with a width proportional to ; multiplying each line by (2 points per liter outside the band) and the output by 10,

which makes no mistakes: the normal trips have and the odd ones . That is 9 parameters. The output rule matters as much as the lines: with (an AND), the same hidden layer flags no trip at all.

§3 · Representation

The hidden space: where one line is enough again

Splitting the network in two, with : the output neuron is a logistic regression whose features are not but . Training the whole network means learning, at the same time, the features (the hidden layer) and the linear classifier on top of them. Whether the problem is easy depends on a single condition: that the classes are linearly separable in the hidden space.

On the band, a logistic regression on is stuck at 12 mistakes (with 18 normal trips and 12 odd ones on both sides, what lowers the cross-entropy the most is almost never raising an alert; the best possible line, which is not the maximum-likelihood one, leaves 6). On , the same logistic regression gives 0. In XOR, the two positive cases land on the same point : the hidden layer has folded the plane along the diagonal.

Since the two lines of the band are parallel, depends only on the deviation , and the image of the whole plane is an L-shaped curve. With non-parallel lines the image is a warped two-dimensional sheet, bounded by the unit square. It is the same idea as the kernel trick of SVMs (mapping the data to a space where a hyperplane is enough), with one difference: there the transformation is chosen in advance, and here it is learned. PCA also changes the space, but with a linear transformation, which cannot fold.

§4 · Width

Parameters, width and universal approximation

Each neuron has one weight per input and one bias, so a network with sizes has

parameters. For one hidden layer of neurons over 2 inputs and one output, ; the 2-2-1 network from §2 has 9. The count is exactly that of sum(p.numel() for p in net.parameters()) in PyTorch (§6).

Closed zones. The normal zone of an engine in (rpm, coolant temperature) is an ellipse with anomalies all around it, in every direction. No line or band encloses it: it takes at least 3 half-spaces (a triangle). With 120 reviewed readings and one hidden layer of sigmoids trained with PyTorch (cross-entropy, Adam and L-BFGS, 12 restarts, inputs standardized with the ellipse), the training error drops to 0 at , and the accuracy on 2,000 new readings levels off around 94–95% from on: the corners of the polygon get rounded off.

def entrenar(m, semillas=12):

        net = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Linear(2, m), torch.nn.Sigmoid(), torch.nn.Linear(m, 1))

Universal approximation theorem. Let be continuous and non-polynomial, compact and continuous. For every there exist and parameters such that

Cybenko (1989) proved it for sigmoids, Hornik (1991) for bounded non-constant activations, and Leshno, Lin, Pinkus and Schocken (1993) gave the exact condition: it is enough (and necessary) that is not a polynomial, which includes ReLU. What it does not say:

  • How many neurons. In general can grow exponentially with and with . Barron (1993) gives a squared error of with no exponential dependence on , but only for functions with a finite Fourier moment .
  • That training will find it. It is an existence result; minimizing the loss is a non-convex problem (topics 5 and 6).
  • Nothing about generalization. Getting close to on with finite data doesn’t guarantee getting new data right, and it says nothing outside .

The speed profile of a 100 km route (simple version, idea 6) illustrates it with the construction typical of the proofs: sigmoids centered apart with a fixed slope, and only the output weights fitted by least squares. The error goes down, but the short stretches (a 4 km village) don’t show up until the steps are finer than they are.

aprox.py · sigmoid steps with output weights fitted by least squares · actual output

import numpy as np
from datos import perfil, KM
from red import sigmoide

v = perfil(KM)                                       # average speed every 500 m on a 100 km route


def ajuste(n):
    """n hidden neurons σ((km − cᵢ)/w): steps spread along the route (cᵢ and w fixed).
    Only the n + 1 output weights are fitted, by least squares."""
    c = (np.arange(n) + 0.5) * 100 / n; w = 0.35 * 100 / n
    B = np.c_[np.ones_like(KM), sigmoide((KM[:, None] - c) / w)]
    a, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(B, v, rcond=None)
    r = B @ a - v
    return np.sqrt(np.mean(r ** 2)), np.max(np.abs(r))


print(" n   mean error     max error  (km/h)")
for n in (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128):
    e, m = ajuste(n)
    print(f"{n:3d}   {e:9.2f}     {m:9.2f}")

§5 · Depth

Composing folds: why depth multiplies

With ReLU, a network is a piecewise linear function. In one dimension, a hidden layer of ReLUs has at most pieces: each neuron contributes one bend, and the bends add up. When layers are composed, on the other hand, the pieces multiply. The tent function

folds onto itself (it rises from 0 to 1 on and falls on ) with 2 neurons. Its composition is a sawtooth with pieces. Since the combination is absorbed into the weights of the next layer, the network has hidden layers of 2 neurons and

parameters (4 in the first layer, from 1 input to 2 neurons; 6 in each layer from 2 to 2; 3 in the output), versus for the single wide layer that would give the same pieces. Telgarsky (2016) uses exactly this construction to prove that there are functions a network of depth represents with constant width and that one of depth can only approximate with width exponential in ; Montúfar, Pascanu, Cho and Bengio (2014) count the linear regions of ReLU networks in dimensions, which grow exponentially with depth. That depth can be exponentially more efficient doesn’t mean every function needs it, or that training takes advantage of that capacity.

profundidad.py · the sawtooth with the MLP class · actual output

import numpy as np
from red import MLP, Densa, n_param


def sierra(L):
    """L hidden layers of 2 ReLU neurons. Each layer computes t(u) = 2·relu(u) − 4·relu(u − ½):
    it folds [0, 1] in half. The combination 2·h1 − 4·h2 goes into the weights of the next layer."""
    capas = [Densa([[1.0], [1.0]], [0.0, -0.5], "relu")]
    capas += [Densa([[2.0, -4.0], [2.0, -4.0]], [0.0, -0.5], "relu") for _ in range(L - 1)]
    return MLP(capas + [Densa([[2.0, -4.0]], [0.0], "identidad")])


def trozos(f, n=2 ** 15):
    u = np.linspace(0, 1, n + 1)[:, None]
    s = np.diff(f(u)[:, 0]) * n                     # slope on each grid interval
    return 1 + int(np.sum(np.abs(np.diff(s)) > 1e-6))


print(" L   pieces   parameters (deep)      one layer: neurons   parameters")
for L in (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10):
    red = sierra(L)
    k = trozos(red.forward)
    assert red.n_param == n_param(red.tamanos) == 6 * L + 1
    m = k - 1                                        # a ReLU layer with m neurons gives at most m + 1 pieces
    print(f"{L:2d}   {k:6d}   {red.n_param:10d}              {m:10d}           {n_param([1, m, 1]):6d}")
print("values at u = 0, 1/8, 1/4, 3/8, 1/2 with L = 3:", sierra(3).forward(np.array([[0], [1 / 8], [1 / 4], [3 / 8], [1 / 2]]))[:, 0])

§6 · From scratch

An MLP class in numpy, verified

Densa (dense layer) stores with shape and computes and ; MLP chains them, checks that the shapes fit, returns the intermediate activations if asked, counts parameters and loads an already trained MLPClassifier (transposing coefs_). There is only a forward pass: gradients and training are topic 5. The sigmoid is computed without overflowing for large , as in topic 1.

red.py

"""A multilayer network (MLP) from scratch in numpy: dense layers = matrix product + bias + activation.
Forward pass only; training (backpropagation) is topic 5."""
import numpy as np


def sigmoide(z):
    z = np.asarray(z, float)
    e = np.exp(-np.abs(z))
    return np.where(z >= 0, 1 / (1 + e), e / (1 + e))


ACT = {"sigmoide": sigmoide, "tanh": np.tanh, "relu": lambda z: np.maximum(z, 0.0),
       "identidad": lambda z: np.asarray(z, float)}


class Densa:
    """One layer: m neurons looking at the same d inputs. W has shape (m, d): one row per neuron."""

    def __init__(self, W, b, act="sigmoide"):
        self.W, self.b, self.act = np.atleast_2d(np.asarray(W, float)), np.asarray(b, float).ravel(), act
        assert self.W.shape[0] == self.b.shape[0], "one row of W and one bias per neuron"

    def __call__(self, X):
        """X: (n, d) → Z = X Wᵀ + b: (n, m) → H = φ(Z). All neurons and all examples at once."""
        Z = np.asarray(X, float) @ self.W.T + self.b
        return Z, ACT[self.act](Z)

    @property
    def n_param(self):
        return self.W.size + self.b.size                # m·d weights + m biases = m·(d + 1)


class MLP:
    def __init__(self, capas):
        self.capas = list(capas)
        for a, b in zip(self.capas, self.capas[1:]):
            assert a.W.shape[0] == b.W.shape[1], "the outputs of one layer are the inputs of the next"

    def forward(self, X, todo=False):
        """Forward pass. With todo=True it also returns (Z, H) for every layer."""
        H, pasos = np.atleast_2d(np.asarray(X, float)), []
        for c in self.capas:
            Z, H = c(H)
            pasos.append((Z, H))
        return (H, pasos) if todo else H

    def predict_proba(self, X):
        p = self.forward(X)[:, 0]
        return np.c_[1 - p, p]                          # same format as scikit-learn: [P(0), P(1)]

    def predict(self, X):
        return (self.forward(X)[:, 0] > 0.5).astype(int)

    @property
    def n_param(self):
        return sum(c.n_param for c in self.capas)

    @property
    def tamanos(self):
        return [self.capas[0].W.shape[1]] + [c.W.shape[0] for c in self.capas]

    @classmethod
    def desde_sklearn(cls, m):
        """MLPClassifier stores coefs_[l] with shape (d, m): the transpose of our W."""
        acts = {"logistic": "sigmoide", "tanh": "tanh", "relu": "relu", "identity": "identidad"}
        L = len(m.coefs_)
        return cls(Densa(W.T, b, acts[m.activation] if l < L - 1 else "sigmoide")
                   for l, (W, b) in enumerate(zip(m.coefs_, m.intercepts_)))


def n_param(tamanos):
    """Parameters of a network with layer sizes [d, m1, m2, …, output]: Σ (inputs + 1) · neurons."""
    return sum((a + 1) * b for a, b in zip(tamanos, tamanos[1:]))

§7 · Checklist

Before using a multilayer network

  • Checked that a linear model is not enough (bands, closed zones, XOR-type interactions); if it is enough, it is simpler and easier to explain.
  • Shapes annotated at every layer: as in torch and in sklearn; batches with the examples in the rows.
  • Parameters counted with and compared with the number of examples.
  • Inputs standardized with training statistics (preparing features): sigmoids saturate with large inputs.
  • Several random restarts: the loss is not convex and each seed can end up at a different solution.
  • Validation on data the network didn’t see: with enough width, any training set can be separated.
  • Your own implementation verified against a reference (torch or sklearn) with the same weights before trusting it.

§8 · Worked example

A 2-2-1 network for the band, built and evaluated by hand

The numbers of the toy and of ideas 1 and 3 of the simple version, computed step by step.

The setup. In the 30 trips from topic 1, normal fuel use is liters per 100 km, with a margin of ±2.5 L. Trip 5 (14.1 t, 37.7 L) is marked as “doesn’t add up”; trip 6 (8.1 t, 28.4 L), as normal. How do you build a network with two hidden neurons that classifies them correctly, and what exactly does it compute for each one?

Example map

We turn the two lines that bound the normal band into two hidden neurons, stack them in a matrix, choose the output neuron’s weights so that it raises an alert if either of them votes, and run the forward pass with an odd trip and a normal one. At the end we look at the 30 trips in the hidden space and count the parameters.

import math
import numpy as np
from datos import carga_consumo
from red import n_param

C, y = carga_consumo()
sig = lambda z: 1 / (1 + math.exp(-z))
k = 2.0                                             # how much the score rises for each liter outside the band
wA, bA = (-0.55 * k, k), -26.5 * k                  # A: fuel use − 0.55·load − 26.5 > 0  (“burns too much”)
wB, bB = (0.55 * k, -k), 21.5 * k                   # B: 21.5 + 0.55·load − fuel use > 0  (“burns too little”)
print(f"2-3) wA = {wA}, bA = {bA}   wB = {wB}, bB = {bB}   σ(k·1 L) = {sig(k):.4f}")
for i in (5, 3, 6):                                 # trip 5 (above), 3 (below), 6 (normal)
    c, q = C[i - 1]
    zA = wA[0] * c + wA[1] * q + bA; zB = wB[0] * c + wB[1] * q + bB

What would change in a real system

  • Learned weights, not hand-set ones: with cross-entropy and backpropagation (topics 4 and 5), and several seeds.
  • More inputs: route grade, average speed, outside temperature. The band becomes a region in dimensions and more neurons will be needed.
  • Standardized inputs, so that the scale of the tonnes and that of the liters don’t skew training.
  • Thousands of trips and validation by week, not 30 trips.
